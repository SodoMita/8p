EIGHT PAGES 1.5.3
Godot 4.7.2 · GL Compatibility · GDScript
----------------------------------------
1.5.3 mouse look, for real this time: 1.5.1 had gated mouse look on Game.touch_mode,
     which _detect_touch() sets for ANY machine reporting a touchscreen -- a
     Windows laptop, a desktop browser with navigator.maxTouchPoints > 0 -- so on
     those PCs the camera could not be turned at all without holding a button.
     Look is now decided by the origin of the event, never by the mode: a real
     mouse always looks, a finger never does (emulation device tag, plus a
     finger-down state so untracked platforms are covered too). Dismissing a note
     also actually dismisses it now: the fade-in tween used to keep running and
     drag it back, eating the next key press as well. 48 selftests + 30 rig + 23
     control checks, the control ones headless and again inside sway where pointer
     capture is real.
1.5.2 effects restored (#28): the camcorder pass had quietly turned the old
     static-grain overlay off and halved the vignette (0.92 -> 0.35) to avoid
     stacking two of each. Both are back at full strength on top of the tape,
     as they were before the camcorder existed; four selftest assertions pin
     them, including across a CAMCORDER FX toggle. Costs ~9 ms of a 497 ms
     llvmpipe frame at quality 1.
1.5.1 control audit: five input bugs fixed and pinned by a new 20-check harness
     (--autotest=controls). A finger produced look twice (touch overlay plus the
     emulated mouse motion from pointing/emulate_mouse_from_touch) so the touch
     camera ran ~1.7x too fast; a free cursor in touch mode turned the camera on
     every move; the touch overlay kept steering and could latch a stick while
     paused or dead; add_look() ignored frozen/paused; and the key that dismisses
     a note also fired flashlight/interact/pause. Pause now silences the overlay,
     the death freeze holds the view still, a real click re-captures the pointer
     on any device, and the cursor free-vs-captured rule is explicit. 34 selftests
     + 30 rig + 20 control checks green.

1.5.2 fence orientation + bark at glancing light.
     * fence panels and gate leaves were rotated 90 degrees: the yaw put the
       plane's WIDTH along the outward normal, so every bay stood sideways to the
       run it belongs to. Panels, rails and their colliders now share one explicit
       basis (`_along_basis`) whose local X lies along the fence line, so all four
       runs and both gate leaves line up. Guarded by a check that every panel's
       local X is perpendicular to its side's outward normal.
     * bark no longer falls to black at steep angles. The albedo was dark to start
       with (0.20 linear) and the normal map steep (18 deg mean tilt), so a
       glancing torch left only specular crumbs. The material now lifts the albedo,
       softens the relief and uses wrapped diffuse so the terminator does not clip
       to zero. Same for the shed's planks.
1.5.1 pages: pinned to real surfaces, dark, and carrying their message.
     * every page is now attached to the thing it belongs to - the face of the
       broken wall, the inside of the tunnel, the silo shell, the truck's side,
       the shed's back wall, a standing stone, the back of the bathroom, and a
       scrap board nailed across the tower's legs - instead of floating in a
       clearing. Each landmark reports a surface point plus an outward normal.
     * no light of their own at all: the per-page OmniLight went earlier, now
       the emission is gone too. Measured against the wall beside it, a page is
       0.81x as bright with the torch on and 0.86x in the IR mode - it is lit,
       not lit-up.
     * the note is on the page. The eight messages are baked into
       assets/textures/note_N.png by tools/gen_notes.py (runtime SubViewport
       capture was tried and is unreliable with eight live viewports), and the
       cloth sheet uses that image as its albedo, so what you read in the world
       is what you collect.
     * the sheet hangs off its nails: top edge pinned, wall-side clamped to the
       surface, calm flutter instead of a flag, two nail heads drawn in.
     * the inverted-face bug is gone and is now guarded: winding and normals
       both point out of the wall, asserted per page in world space.
     * map_builder: page spots carry a normal; a wall page used to be invisible
       from the front because the visible face was a back face.
1.5  the map is closed and the world outside it keeps going.
     * boundary: a real chain-link fence, built the way one is built — 3 m
       mesh bays, galvanised posts, top rail, bottom tension wire, and a
       two-leaf gate standing open at the east side. 325 colliders on the
       ring, checked arithmetically (ring closure, per-run coverage, gate
       clearance) instead of by walking a character into it.
     * outside: endless procedural forest streamed in 32 m chunks around the
       player, plus a crowned dirt road that leaves through the gate and
       wanders to the horizon. A 4 km floor slab means there is no edge to
       fall off even if you go over the wire.
     * pages are verlet cloth now: 7x9 pinned grid, fixed 60 Hz, shear links,
       smoothed live normals, light coming through the sheet — and they carry
       no light of their own.
     * concrete/stone moved to world-space triplanar at 1.2 m per repeat:
       2048² maps at ~1700 px/m on every face, no stretch (was up to 46:1),
       isotropically rebuilt maps (runoff streaks and the per-tile moss band
       removed, aggregate-led tone), mipmapped.
     * tree normals re-verified the hard way: every triangle is wound with
       its vertex normals, and every sampled face passes a step-out-and-shoot
       -back test; the black caps at trunk bases were the downward-facing base
       fans showing above ground, so trees now sit 12 cm into the soil.
     * two deepseek audit agents ran in parallel during this cycle; all eleven
       of their cloth/terrain findings are fixed. 38 selftest checks.
